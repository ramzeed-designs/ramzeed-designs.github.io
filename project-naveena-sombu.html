Naveena Sombu

a.k.a. the modern lota. Spit happens!

A one hand spit container for drivers who chew pan or gutka, so the spit goes into the lota instead of onto the road.

01The problem

Redwashing.

The Sombu (Tamil) or Lota (Hindi) is one of India's most familiar designed objects, in use since the 2nd millennium BC for rituals, drinking water and storing liquids. Over time it picked up one more use: a pot to spit pan into.

Could the iconic lota be redesigned for this one extreme use?

02The brief

Design for an extreme user.

An extreme user sits at the far ends of a product's usual user spectrum: they use it more intensely, more often, or in unconventional ways than the average person.

03Discover

First, break our own structure.

For most of us this was the first time treating design as a process. We kept falling back on a tidy, engineering-style approach, and it showed.

  1. Try 1

    Domains first

    We listed domains (healthcare, transport, food…) and tried to force extreme scenarios out of each. The A1 sheet stayed almost empty.

  2. Try 2

    Our own lives

    Our mentor told us to stop boxing ideas into domains and write in every direction, in different colours. We also looked at our own and our classmates' life experiences.

  3. Try 3

    Situations first

    We paired short user personas with situations, then asked if there was a problem. It got rid of "that's a you problem" and let us see things from the user's side.

04Define

From 21, to 7, to 1.

We contacted people living each situation and cut statements on two things: could we reach these users in person this week, and did they even see it as a problem?

Ranking the final 7

A mentor, Nikhil, introduced us to the priority matrix. We ranked each statement on four parameters.

  • Societal impact
  • Width of users
  • Criticality of solution
  • User accessibility
  • Long nails × touch typing
  • Redwashing × driverspicked!
  • Sneaker fans × bikers
  • Hoarding × ADHD
  • Deaf students × school
  • Eyewear × sports
  • Tinnitus × workspaces

our final HMW

How might we help car drivers who chew pan or gutka while travelling dispose of their spit safely, while reducing the disgust it causes for others on the road and in the car?

05Re-discovery

Wait. Did we rush that?

The decision felt rushed by the deadline, and if it was wrong, the whole second week would be wasted. With another mentor, Kabyashree, backing us, we went back to Discover and Define, this time digging only into redwashing. We came out with three extreme-user scenarios and carried all three forward.

  • Scenario 1

    Car drivers

    Who chew pan on the move and spit out of the car, leaving a mess for others on the road and in the car.

  • Scenario 2

    Bike riders

    Who need to spit while wearing a helmet, without spraying the people around them.

  • Scenario 3

    Around dustbins

    Where redwashing makes people avoid using the bin at all.

the metaphor: an invisibility cloak

We weren't trying to stop people from eating pan. We wanted the spit to disappear safely, so both the person chewing and everyone around them are safe.

06Develop

50+ sketches, against the clock.

Getting ideas out of our heads had been our weak spot. A time-boxed sketch sprint fixed that. We split the three scenarios 3–3–2 between us, then drew for any scenario whenever an idea came up.

a few of the 50+ sketches

Combine, don't cut.

We scored ideas on disposability, recyclability, cost, storage efficiency and ease of use. Then, instead of throwing out the low scorers, we grouped ideas that worked well together or shared a principle, and merged them into stronger ones.

08The final product

Grip, flip, spit, shut.

  • gripA curved body we tested in thermocol and terracotta so it sits right in one hand.
  • flipThe cap and hinge sit where one hand can flip them open, so the other stays on the wheel.
  • shutThe cap closes over the mouth, so the spit stays inside the lota and off the road.

09Our process

Not quite a double diamond.

wiggly startWe had no direction until we understood what an extreme user is. Once that clicked, Discover went smoothly.

the spiralDefine was rushed and left us unsure, so we went back and re-discovered and re-defined three related HMWs in parallel.

back on trackWe merged them into one, then sailed through Develop and Deliver, with a detour into materials on the way.

10Outcome

On the wall, and on the table.

At the end-of-course show we learnt from our batchmates' work and got valuable feedback on what to improve next.

What I took away

  • Time-bound thinking: knowing where to stop and when to revisit.
  • How to run user interviews and get to the key points quickly.
  • Explaining ideas clearly, in a team and in formal reviews.
  • Still learning: being creative within limits of time, resources and deliverables.
