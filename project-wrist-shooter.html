01THE BACKGROUND

I wasn't given a brief. So I made one.

During my internship I was asked to explore opportunities for expanding Smartivity's STEM DIY toy portfolio. Instead of waiting for a predefined project, I started by asking three questions.

What play styles were missing for 6+ age groups?

What kinds of toys are kids this age reaching for, and why?

What have competitors done that Smartivity hasn't?

02RESEARCH

WHAT THE SURVEY ALSO TOLD ME

Kids get bored fast

"Boring after short time" was the #1 frustration mentioned by the majority of parents.

Superheroes & space rule

Top themes were superheroes, space, and building. All pointing toward action and imagination.

One time use = dealbreaker

Parents want replay value. A toy that's done after one session doesn't justify the cost.

Active play wins

Most kids play actively, i.e running, moving, playing with others. Desk-bound toys have limited appeal.

The direction was clear:
build the wearable.

(parents voted,
not me!)

06PROTOTYPING

Build it, break it, fix it.

I started with some rough prototypes constantly changing things,

A.

The arm unit

Tried: clamping foldable lock

Broke: It was supposed to fail as its mechanical

Fixed: switched the lock to velcro, simple and kid-proof.

Velcro made it better and also reduced the cost too

Why: Sizing differences were now not an issue

drawback: little bit increase in time to wear/play

Fixed: made it shorter and easy to slip on to

B.

The Shooter

Tried: Shooter mechanism with the trigger and firing areas exposed.

Broke: Accidental firing occurred, and the darts would fall out when kids ran.

Fixed: Developed a new mechanism that holds the Nerf darts securely and transfers force more effectively.

now darts can sit better, and it shoots gliders too

Why: The foam darts can be inserted directly onto the plug.

drawback: It can only shoot one dart or glider at a time

Fixed: Redesigned the mechanism so both darts and gliders can be shot. I also designed a glider with a Nerf dart attachment.

C.

A claw you control with your wrist

Tried: A claw using a scissor mechanism that could be operated with one hand.

Broke: The mechanism was not intuitive to use.

Fixed: Redesigned the mechanism so that wrist movement opens the claw, while moving the wrist down closes it, made it easier and more intuitive to grab

07USER TESTING

Then I handed it to the real experts.

"It’s really fun, I love it”

Prayansh 8

Observed: The toy goes through rough use during active play.
Changed: Swapped PVC for MDF in the parts that can bend or experience stress.

Observed: If the strap fails, the toy cannot shoot.
Changed: Added an extra firing button at the back, allowing it to shoot accurately.

Observed: Adults were interested in trying the toy.
Changed: Tweaked the dimensions of the arm unit.

09OUTCOME

From a self-written brief to a sales-approved toy.

The project was handed over to a senior designer along with my suggestions and feedback. The design evolved through my involvement at different stages, and it is now set to launch in October 2026.

WHAT I LEARNED IN THIS JOURNEY

1Design evolves at every stage, and good design comes from collaboration.

2User testing is really important at every stage because it gives you insights you might otherwise miss

3Early failure gives us useful feedback sooner
