01The context

A good library, a lot of walking.

The KMC already has an RFID kiosk, email reminders and an online catalogue. Yet students still walk in to check if a book is there, note down call numbers to hunt for on the shelves, and forget the titles faculty recommend. We asked how the library's existing automation could lower the cognitive load on readers and librarians alike.

our objectives

  • User research and primary research
  • Apply UX principles and theories
  • Find and solve real pain points

03Insights

The hunt is the hard part.

86%

visit the KMC once a week or more

79%

say the email reminders help them return on time

50%

search by call number, a string most people have to write down

What makes finding a book hard?

14 responses, more than one answer allowed

  • Knowing if the book is missing from the shelf71%
  • Finding the section (Art, Psychology…)64%
  • Noting the book number and searching for it50%
  • Locating the book on the shelf36%
  • Finding the right book in the online catalogue36%

What students told us

  1. They want to browse the catalogue remotely, without walking to the library.
  2. Classes run at odd hours, so they want to reserve a book and collect it when it suits them.
  3. They want to read what faculty suggest, but forget to note the names down.
  4. Finding department-wise books is a task in itself.
  5. Locating a book on the shelf is hard.

05User flow

One sign-in, four journeys.

Readers sign in with email, Facebook or Google. From there the app branches four ways, each built for a different need: discovering what's trending, keeping up with issues and due dates, searching for a specific title, and sharing what you thought of a book.

  • Hometrending book → reserve
  • Notificationsissued, due, renewed
  • Searchfind → reserve
  • Reviewsrate → read others
06Wireframes

Rough first, in Balsamiq.

Quick low-fidelity screens let us argue about structure before colour: a home carousel, an account menu, My Books with due dates and a "pay fine" shortcut, a wishlist with reminders, semester- and module-wise book lists, and a readers' forum.

07The UI

Every screen answers an insight.

Insights 1 & 4

The catalogue, sorted the way NID thinks.

Browse by department, then switch between all books and the ones specific to your modules. Search works by voice too, so nobody needs to copy a call number.

Insight 2 · persona frustrations

Reserve now, collect within 24 hours.

If someone already has it, Trove says who, so you can ask to share, and offers to wishlist it and ping you when it's back. After reading, a quick review helps the next person decide.

79% like reminders

Deadlines you can't miss.

My Books shows what you have and since when, fines are one tap from your account, and notifications cover issued, last-day, renewed and new arrivals.
